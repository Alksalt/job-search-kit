# What the original search taught us

[Norsk bokmål](../nb/experience.md)

Written on 27 September 2026. This is one person's experience searching for work in Norway with a private workflow using GPT/Codex and Claude. It is the background to this project, not an evaluation of the public toolkit or a forecast of anyone else's results. The creator has chosen to share these aggregate outcomes; they are attributable, not an anonymous dataset. Underlying records remain private.

## Many rejections, and progress in several directions

Progress took hundreds of applications. There were many rejections, unanswered applications and processes that the applicant chose to leave. There were also invitations across care and social support, software and AI, and research support and administration.

| Documented outcome as of writing | Count |
| --- | ---: |
| Applications, mainly to advertised vacancies | More than 300 |
| Records classified as rejected, including some administrative closures | More than 100 |
| Distinct recruitment processes with an interview or vacancy-related conversation invitation | At least 9 |
| Processes with a documented completed interview | 5 |
| Processes that produced a job offer | 2 |

The last three rows overlap: offers came from interviewed processes, and completed interviews came from invitations. Four invitations were declined before an interview. Two offers do not mean two accepted jobs or two signed contracts. Some other processes were still unresolved at the reporting date.

The broad application and rejection totals are deliberately rounded. Historical records contain duplicates, missing dates and some administrative closures. The rejection row is not an audited count of explicit employer rejection messages. Silence, candidate withdrawals and vacancies screened out before applying are not counted as employer rejections. The nine invitation processes were checked individually and deduplicated; a video-screening request and generic receipt acknowledgements were excluded. These records do not support a precise success rate.

## Advertised vacancies and unsolicited outreach

A separate audit dated 22 September covered outreach to **267 organisations**, mostly cold contacts with a few warm introductions. It documented **7 conversations held or concretely offered**, including conversations arising from introductions. These were networking outcomes, not seven completed hiring interviews. That audit documented **no hiring interviews or formal job offers attributable to this outreach cohort**. Its cutoff is earlier than the application figures above; it is not a fresh mailbox check through 27 September.

The documented hiring interviews and both offers came from applications to concrete vacancies. The outreach audit recorded conversations, referrals and useful information. Based on this experience, the practical choice for a similar search would be to prioritise suitable advertised roles and use selective open applications and warm introductions as a targeted complement. That is a judgment about where to focus effort, not a measured ranking of channels.

This does not establish that open applications fail in Norway. An advertised vacancy signals an existing hiring need; unsolicited outreach may develop contacts whose value appears later. The two channels differed in target roles, employers, timing and follow-up. Their summaries count different units and outcomes: do not add them together or turn them into comparative success rates. Outreach also commonly included a CV and letter, so the comparison says nothing about whether a CV alone beats a cover letter. Nor can it identify which AI provider helped more, prove that automation caused an offer, or measure time saved.

## Lessons to use

- Keep a date and source for invitations, completed interviews and offers in your private tracker. Define the categories before counting, remove duplicates and date every snapshot. A current status such as “withdrawn” can hide an earlier invitation or offer.
- Start with roles whose actual requirements fit your documented experience. Make that relevance easy to see in the CV and application material the employer requests.
- Use outreach to learn about a field or develop contacts, and count that value separately from hiring progress. A polite reply or a saved CV is not an interview.
- Applications sent measure effort. Count invitations, interviews and offers separately to describe progress, and keep rejections distinct from unanswered applications.

NAV advises adapting both the CV and application to the role. Its guidance also recognises open applications as a legitimate option. These sources support relevant, targeted applications; they do not provide a national conversion-rate comparison.
